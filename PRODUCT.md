# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

East Texas homeowners (Jacksonville and Palestine areas) who own a residential pool and are researching pool care providers. They're in comparison mode: reading about the company, assessing expertise, and building enough trust to pick up the phone or submit a form. The context is casual browsing from home, usually on mobile. They have a specific problem (green pool, broken pump, seasonal opening) or are tired of maintaining it themselves.

## Product Purpose

Diamond D Pool Services is a local pool maintenance and repair business serving Jacksonville and Palestine, TX. The site's job is to convert cautious browsers into confident callers: establish credibility, communicate the range of services clearly, and make contact friction-free. Success is a phone call or quote request.

## Brand Personality

Expert, calm, reliable. The seasoned pro who has seen every pool problem and handles it without drama. Trustworthy because of competence, not because of marketing language. Warmth comes through in the local specificity and direct communication, not through forced friendliness.

## Anti-references

- Generic franchise pool service sites (Leslie's, blue-and-white corporate look, stock photography of smiling technicians)
- Overdesigned luxury spa aesthetic (marble textures, chandelier photography, gold accents, "resort lifestyle" framing)
- Outdated local contractor sites (clip art, busy layouts, no hierarchy, 2005-era web design)
- Pushy sales/lead-gen sites (pop-ups, urgency countdowns, CALL NOW in red, excessive badge clusters)

## Design Principles

1. **Trust through restraint.** Confidence doesn't need to announce itself. Clean hierarchy, deliberate spacing, and precise copy signal competence more reliably than superlatives.
2. **Local over generic.** Mention the towns. Use real service language. Avoid category-bucket phrases (seamless, professional-grade, top-rated) in favor of what Diamond D literally does and where.
3. **The work speaks.** Show pools and results, not abstract claims. Photography and process details earn trust; promises don't.
4. **Clarity earns calls.** Every section should reduce a visitor's hesitation. If copy doesn't answer a likely question or move toward contact, cut it.
5. **Craft signals quality.** A well-made site signals a well-run service. Typographic care, considered motion, and consistent detail all read as evidence of professionalism.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Ensure all body text meets 4.5:1 contrast against background. Support reduced-motion preference (already wired in CSS). Mobile-first: primary user context is a phone, possibly one-handed, often outdoors in Texas sunlight (high ambient light — contrast headroom matters more than average).
